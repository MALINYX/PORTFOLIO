02 — The design challenge

Every refill ships the hardware again.

Each repeat purchase of a fine fragrance ships the glass, pump, cap and collar again. RF03-U1 splits the object in two. A permanent atomizer keeps the spray engine, the manifold, a short probe and the bottom mechanism. A sealed cartridge carries the reservoir, the valve, the fitment and the full-length dip tube. Only the docking geometry between them would be standardised.

04 — Design response5 decisions · reason · evidence

The decisions that shaped it.

Decision 01

The cartridge never rotates

Rotation at the interface would wipe the seal, so the design avoids it: the user turns the bottom cap, and a three-ramp cam turns that into purely axial travel.

Designed

Decision 02

Seal before open

Designed so the outer seal engages before the valve opens. On the way out the order reverses: the valve closes before the seal lets go.

Designed · state order

Decision 03

A short permanent wet path

Reservoir, valve, fitment and the full-length dip tube leave with the cartridge; the atomizer keeps only the spray engine, the manifold and a short probe.

Designed

Decision 04

The load bypasses the glass

Designed so the carrier loads the cartridge’s rigid base foot; the glass is never a bearing or a cam reaction member.

Designed

Decision 05

Share only the interface

Standardise the fitment, valve, probe axis and key; leave scent, graphics and authentication to each house. Cross-brand adoption is a hypothesis, not a claim.

Specified intent · hypothesis
05 — Form developmentWhat changed, and why

Directions set aside, and why.

Directions set aside

Each for a mechanical or material reason, not a stylistic one: a paper pouch with a piercing straw (a sharp probe cannot reseal); a screw-in rotating cartridge (rotation reaches the seal); a long reusable dip tube (the largest wet path stays in service); an aluminium probe chosen for looks (appearance cannot select a formula-contact material).

How it is built.

Before any filled test: the reference formula supplied for compatibility testing listed CAS 623-19-8 as its solvent, but that number is furfuryl propionate, not the intended IPM or DPG. Rather than guess, every raw material must be confirmed by the formula supplier; formula-filled testing is on hold until then, and none has run.

Engineering notes the longer story

As designed, the cap turns 60° and the cartridge rises 4 mm without rotating; the seal engages before the valve opens, and on the way out the valve closes before the seal releases. These are the designed sequence and starting targets, not measured behaviour.

Nothing here is validated: only a 3D-printed form model exists, for form and handling. The test rig that would measure travel, torque and the valve stroke is specified, not built.
