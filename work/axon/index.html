02 — Origin

2017 → 2026

AXON is a name I've carried for more than fifteen years, first as a business idea. The lamp came in 2017: one ring that sets height, tilt and direction. In October 2026 I engineered it, from kinematics to a frozen, checked CAD.

04 — Why a cam

Three mechanisms, one input

All three held. The cam was the lightest to turn.

A cam, a gear train and a linkage were each modelled to do the same job. All three hold every pose by geometry, so holding did not decide it: the cam needs 254 N·mm at the ring against 385 and 399, and it is the only one that nests at STOWED with no collision inside the mass target. Its cost is the part count.

05 — How it holds

By geometry

No brake. No balance spring.

Each shoe rests on a groove flank cut 10.29° steep, under the 11.31° friction angle, so the head's weight cannot turn the ring. The detents only click and the end stops only stop: neither holds a pose.

Specified intent · provisional friction

The angles and why the margin is provisional

The flank is 10.29° against a friction angle of 11.31° at the provisional μ 0.20: a holding margin of 1.02°. The selection rule asks for at least 2° at the lowest measured μ, so the groove lead is chosen after the friction coupons are measured; the band holds down to μ 0.184. Lifting the detent ball takes 89 N·mm at the ring.

06 — Found and fixed

Caught in the model, not in a print

Three problems the model caught.

The split drum

Problem

80.2° overhang

The two shoes' loads point opposite ways at every lead and angle, so in a one-piece drum one of the two locking flanks would print face-down.

Fix

≤ 45° overhang

Two bands, ring A printed upside down and ring B upright, each locking flank face-up, joined at a lapped seam.

Designed · model

The floor plate

Problem

0.48 mm sag

A 2.5 mm printed floor plate under a 2.0 kg lamp sinks 0.30–0.48 mm at the rim and closes ring B's 0.5 mm running gap.

Fix

16 µm

The same plate in 6061-T6, 23–34× as stiff in bending: the rim gap stays at least 0.48 mm, for 73 g more.

Designed · model

Rack B's backing roller

Problem

4.3 mm off the mesh

A 5 N push on the stowed head bent rack B's blade away from the tilt pinion: the head front moved 13.7 mm and the teeth parted past their working depth.

Fix

1.92 of 2 mm engaged

A flanged ball bearing on a dowel behind rack B, opposite the mesh: the deflection under the same push is 1.65 mm (base held), against a 2 mm target.

Specified intent · model

What gets measured next, and how

The pass criteria were written down before any reading, and the analysers stay frozen until real data arrives.

The tests and their pass criteria fixed before data

TP-02 · fits ladders

Clearance and press ladders, each bracketing its fit; a ladder that does not bracket is reprinted, never extrapolated.

Specified intent · test protocol

TP-01 · flank friction

2942 g on the shoes (9.62 N per shoe). Pass: run-in μ spread ≤ 0.06 with CV ≤ 15 %; |μ run-in − μ fresh| ≤ 0.05; no cracking or transfer; the lever end moves ≤ 1 mm under hold.

Specified intent · test protocol

The selection rule

Holding margin ≥ 2° at the lowest corrected μ; lamp ≤ 2000 g; nested gap ≤ 15 mm. The rule sets the lead; the 12 lead-dependent parts are made after it.

Specified intent · fixed before data

Phase 3 · the prototype

Free play R-12a ≤ 0.5 mm and R-12b ≤ 17 mm; deflection ≤ 2 mm under 5 N; the base walk at desk μ ≥ 0.6; the detent's breakout 1.5–2×.
