03 — The design challenge

A head that locks, seals and shows it.

The concentrator carries the airflow, so the interface must hold it, seal the Ø28 mm air path and make its lock state readable. The handle was developed in controlled 3D geometry iterations — proportion, bridge, interface, concentrator, CMF — each frozen after a side-by-side comparison, so later decisions never reopened earlier ones.

05 — Design response5 decisions · reason · evidence

The decisions that shaped it.

Decision 01

Three-lug bayonet

Three lugs at 120° spacing give the interface rotational symmetry. A separate 30° turn locks the attachment and draws it 0.50 mm down onto the seal.

Specified intent · modelled, never built

Decision 02

A lock you can read

Amber arcs sit behind two 28° side windows while the attachment is unlocked and swing clear when it locks, so the lock state reads at a glance.

Specified intent

Decision 03

Air through the interface

A Ø28 mm air path runs clear through the collar; contact pins (Ø1.5) sit recessed 1.2 mm under a 0.5 mm wall.

Specified intent

Decision 04

A fork, not a bolt-on

The bridge emerges from under the collar and joins into one root — a Y-crotch — that never exceeds the collar’s Ø47.

Designed

Decision 05

A geometric thermal break

A void separates the 2.6 mm shell from the 2.0 mm duct wall, with a minimum clearance of +0.251 mm. It is geometry only: no thermal performance is claimed.

Designed · geometric only
06 — Form developmentWhat changed, and why

Iterations that explain the final geometry.

Proportion

Before any detail, the first base was re-proportioned: collar depth 15 → 10 mm, front transition 13.5 → 10 mm, control spine 114.5 → 100 mm.

Bridge

Five bridge variants were compared under identical cameras. B1 was short (18 mm penetration, 2 × 9.5 mm legs); B2 long (30 mm), with a central field. The selected B2.2 fork joins its legs into one root under the collar, with a 0.70 mm shadow gap.

Concentrator length

Only the transition length varied. 75 mm compresses the three cones into one dome; 90 mm drifts toward a bottle silhouette; 82 mm keeps the rate changes legible — locked.

Shoulder

Three shoulders on the same 7.5 mm zone — crisp, balanced and soft. The balanced one, a 2.8 mm land and a 3.4 mm eased facet, was selected.

Engineering notes the longer story

THERMA is a 40 × 36 × 155 mm handle with a detachable airflow attachment. Every key dimension is a variable, and every stage was frozen only after a side-by-side comparison: five bridge variants led to one fork, three interface iterations to a three-lug bayonet, three concentrator lengths to 82 mm.

Behind the exterior sits a geometry study, revised through several review rounds, of whether the frozen shell can hold a real product: a lateral clamshell split, a lofted cavity and a register of conflicts. Flow, heat, power, noise and safety are not established.
