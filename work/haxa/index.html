02 — The design challenge

The original could not exist.

HAXĀ began as a KeyShot centrepiece. As modelled, the case was solid, the crystal was a 0.22 mm sheet, the hands overlapped in height and nothing sat inside. The 2026 model had to keep every visible surface and make room for real watchmaking.

Original render and 2026 model — different cameras and lighting, so compare the geometry, not the mood.

04 — Design response4 decisions · reason · evidence

The decisions that shaped it.

Decision 01

Keep every visible surface

2,118 case and 771 bezel vertices lie on the new surfaces. Dial and movement sink 1.668 mm instead of shaving the 60 pillars.

Measured on model

Decision 02

A real calibre inside

A Sellita SW300-1 automatic, modelled inside its published envelope: 71 parts. Internal geometry is representative where the data sheet stops.

Supplier spec · Sellita

Decision 03

Sapphire under the bezel

A 1.20 mm sapphire is fitted from inside under a 0.50 mm bezel lip; the caseback steps 0.97 mm below the original case.

Measured on model

Decision 04

Crown on the stem axis

The crown moves down 0.168 mm onto the calibre’s stem axis; the hands are regrouped and restacked on Sellita heights.

Measured on model

Engineering notes the longer story

HAXĀ began as a portfolio centrepiece: a sculpted Persian winged figure on a hexagon field, rendered in KeyShot. It looked like a watch, but it was a solid case with a 0.22 mm sheet for a crystal and no movement inside.

In 2026 I re-engineered it around a real movement. The visible surfaces stay exactly as I drew them — 2,118 case and 771 bezel vertices lie on the new surfaces — and the inside is new: a Sellita SW300-1 automatic modelled to its published envelope, a 1.20 mm sapphire under a 0.50 mm bezel lip, a crown on the stem axis, and hands restacked on the calibre's heights.
