02 — The design challenge

Too small to fill, too open to hold.

At its drawn size the flacon held 2.28 ml, its glass had no thickness and the bottle passed straight through its holder. The 2026 model finds one scale that makes every wall real and keeps the proportions.

Original render and 2026 model — different cameras and lighting, so compare the geometry, not the mood.

04 — Design response4 decisions · reason · evidence

The decisions that shaped it.

Decision 01

One scale for everything

Flacon, stopper and coffret scale together by ×2.505, set by the thinnest wall reaching 2.0 mm.

Designed

Decision 02

A splash extrait, not a spray

The neck land is only 13.9 mm deep — less than a 15 mm spray ferrule needs — so the cap became a ground stopper.

Measured on model

Decision 03

Glass with real walls

The glass solid is the outer skin minus the inner skin, pushed out to 2.1 mm wherever the body was thinner (sampled; the neck wall is 1.81 mm): 33.2 ml brimful for a 30 ml fill.

Measured on model

Decision 04

A holder the bottle sits in

The holder is raised onto the stand and pocketed for the bottle with 0.40 mm clearance.

Measured on model

Engineering notes the longer story

ABAN was a bell-shaped flacon with a gold swirl emblem and an ornate stopper, presented in velvet coffrets in four editions. The original model had open glass surfaces, no neck, and a bottle passing straight through its holder.

The 2026 model uses one scale factor for flacon, stopper and coffret (×2.505), set by the thinnest wall reaching 2.0 mm. At the neck the bell is too shallow for a spray ferrule, so it became a splash extrait with a ground stopper — the proportions stay mine.
