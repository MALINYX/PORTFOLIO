01 — The projectECS

My original carry system for e-bike commuters, re-engineered in 2026: a load body of about 20.5 L outer volume on side rails and a spine pivot that let it move as the rider leans. The mechanism keeps its documented principle, tension-shortening, and is measured rather than tuned to its targets.

ECS was designed as a wearable load-transfer system for urban e-bike commuters. Under forward lean, a tension-shortening harness is meant to pull the load body down and in toward the spine, shortening the lever between hip and load. The original documentation describes it in ten component sheets (A1–A10), kinematic sheets and a cardboard mock-up.

The 2026 rebuild keeps my original design and takes component dimensions from the A1–A10 sheets where they exist. Where the design goes further than the sheets (the Ø52 gunmetal hubs on satin arms in moulded side plates, the slotted spine bar, the moulded TPU side patches, the copper-stitched shoulder straps that split into two legs onto the belt wings), r3 follows the design and records the change.

Then the mechanism was measured as documented in principle. At a 35° riding lean the load body slides 11.6 mm (9.9 mm vertically) and does not move inward. The sheets set a 100–150 mm drop and call that target conceptual. On a bike, the −40 % lever change comes from the rider's posture, not the product; the mechanism itself lengthens the hip lever slightly (+4.3 %). That gap is the next design problem, and it is shown here as measured.

04 — Decisions & open itemsHonest about what is not done

Decisions

  • 01My original design kept; dimensions from the A1–A10 sheets where they exist, changes recorded on sheet 3.
  • 02Mechanism kept to its documented principle (tension-shortening) and measured, not tuned to reach its targets.
  • 03Interference screen (unintended contacts) covers the upright, riding and packshot poses: soft parts against the form, and against rigid parts in the packshot.

Still open

  • 01Slide of 11.6 mm against the documented 100–150 mm drop, and 0 mm inward against 60–100 mm: the mechanism as drawn does not reach its targets.
  • 02Hip moment arm +4.3 % (140.8 → 146.9 mm), locked → working, against a documented −30 to −40 % (conceptual).
  • 03A4 and A6 sit hidden inside the spine strip and the shell, as in the design: a change to the documented layout, still to confirm.
  • 04Sternum span of 188.5 mm, outside the sheet's 200–210 mm.
  • 05Estimated masses differ from the sheets: back panel ≈ 299 g against 110 g stated, spine rail ≈ 70 g against 28 g.
  • 06Still to decide, where my design views differ: hub height and size (Ø52, or Ø31–39 in the side view), sternum height, belt depth, and the back silhouette (top handle, side hubs, one strap leg, the top loop).
