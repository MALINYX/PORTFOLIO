01 — The product in 10 secondsECS

Carry that moves with the rider.

What it is
A backpack-format carry system for e-bike commuters: a load body of about 20.5 L on side rails, a spine pivot and a harness that splits onto belt wings.
Who it is for
Urban e-bike commuters riding with a load.
The problem
When the rider leans forward, the load’s lever on the hips should get shorter.
What is different
A tension-shortening harness is meant to pull the load down and in. Measured on the 2026 model, it slides 11.6 mm against a 100–150 mm target.

Engineering notes the longer story

ECS was designed as a wearable load-transfer system for urban e-bike commuters. Under forward lean, a tension-shortening harness is meant to pull the load body down and in toward the spine, shortening the lever between hip and load. The original documentation describes it in ten component sheets (A1–A10), kinematic sheets and a cardboard mock-up.

The 2026 model keeps my original design and takes component dimensions from the A1–A10 sheets where they exist. Where the design goes further than the sheets (the Ø52 gunmetal hubs on satin arms in moulded side plates, the slotted spine bar, the moulded TPU side patches, the copper-stitched shoulder straps that split into two legs onto the belt wings), the 2026 model follows the design and records the change.

Then the mechanism was measured, as documented. At a 35° riding lean the load body slides 11.6 mm (9.9 mm vertically) and does not move inward. The sheets set a 100–150 mm drop and call that target conceptual. On a bike, the −40 % lever change comes from the rider's posture, not the product; the mechanism itself lengthens the hip lever slightly (+4.3 %). That gap is the next design problem, and it is shown here as measured.

Not claimed
  • Load-transfer or comfort performance
  • Strap, fabric or joint strength
  • Manufacture
  • Prototype validation, although the status table in the original documentation says so: the physical evidence is a cardboard form mock-up only
Still open
  • Slide of 11.6 mm against the documented 100–150 mm drop, and 0 mm inward against 60–100 mm: the mechanism as drawn does not reach its targets.
  • Hip moment arm +4.3 % (140.8 → 146.9 mm), locked → working, against a documented −30 to −40 % (conceptual).
  • The pivot (A4) and tension strap (A6) sit hidden inside the spine strip and the shell, as in the design: a change to the documented layout, still to confirm.
  • Sternum span of 188.5 mm, outside the sheet's 200–210 mm.
  • Estimated masses differ from the sheets: back panel ≈ 299 g against 110 g stated, spine rail ≈ 70 g against 28 g.
  • Still to decide, where my design views differ: hub height and size (Ø52, or Ø31–39 in the side view), sternum height, belt depth, and the back silhouette (top handle, side hubs, one strap leg, the top loop).
